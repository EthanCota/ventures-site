What it is

Beachhead Intel builds a capability-tagged roster of every reachable business in a city×vertical — not just names and addresses (that's a solved, commoditized problem) but what equipment, capability, or service line each one actually runs, pulled from public state business registries, open map data, and each business's own website. It's the same method that produced the working proof of concept: portland-signs, a 1,463-business dataset of Portland-area sign, print, and graphics shops built in a single day for zero metered tool cost .

The output is a static report or a standing subscription — a roster, ranked and tagged, that tells a territory seller who's really out there, what they run, and where the manual-prospecting blind spots are.

Who it's for

Territory sellers

Independent manufacturer's reps, distributor and dealer sales managers, and franchise-development staff who must answer “who's really in city X that could buy vertical Y, and what do they already run” before they can prospect — today doing it by manual Maps browsing or paying for firmographic-only databases that don't carry equipment or capability data at all.

Dealer network sales reps

Equipment-dealer sales reps (the flagship case: a wide-format printing equipment dealer network) who need a ranked, city-scoped prospect list of shops with the right capability tags — the full addressable universe, not just existing owners of one brand — plus displacement targets against competitors already on the floor.

Not for: enterprise sales orgs already running ZoomInfo or D&B enterprise tiers with dedicated market-intel staff — they've solved this a different, more expensive way. Not a consumer data product — this is business-entity data only.

Tools & features

Capability tagging

Every business gets tagged by what it actually does and runs — wide-format, vehicle wraps, laser engraving, DTF, dye-sub, CNC and more — extracted from public website text, not guessed from category codes.

Geographic-gap detection

City-by-city sweep against the state business registry, not a fixed-radius circle from downtown — catches the edge-of-metro businesses that radius-based prospecting structurally misses.

Displacement targeting

Flags businesses that already run a competitor's equipment in a given capability class — a ranked replacement-sale target list, not just a cold-prospect list.

Dealer briefing sheets

For channel motions: a per-rep, per-territory sheet turning the raw roster into a workable call list with capability context attached.

Questions we expect

Isn't this just scraped data you're reselling illegally, like every lead database that gets sued?

No — and the distinction is specific, not a hand-wave. Sources here are state open-data business registries (public government records, no platform terms apply), OpenStreetMap (ODbL, explicitly permits reuse with attribution), and individual business websites under robots.txt / rate-limit discipline. Google Maps/Places and Facebook/Instagram — the two platforms whose terms actually bar this kind of commercial resale — are named, permanent non-sources and are not used.

How good is your equipment-detection data, really — did you find our competitor's customers?

Honestly: only 4.9% of reachable sites (35 of 713) name any equipment brand at all, and only 11 clearly say they operate it. The sellable value isn't “we found everyone's exact machine” — it's the much larger capability-tagged addressable universe (wide-format, wraps, laser, DTF, dye-sub, CNC) ranked for prospecting, including displacement targets.

How is this different from ZoomInfo, Apollo, D&B, or Regrid?

None of those sell physical-equipment or capability data as a line item — they sell who a business is and how to contact it. This is a different data category entirely, not a cheaper version of the same one.

Why pay $750–$1,500 for a one-off report when Infofree is $150–$275/mo?

Infofree sells contact and firmographic lists — the same category as Apollo/ZoomInfo, just priced for self-serve. It doesn't tag capability or equipment data at all. The report is a different product, priced on build-value, and the Standing Subscription tier exists for buyers who want Infofree's recurring shape with this data instead.

What's in this for a dealer network specifically — aren't you just another vendor pitch?

The capability-universe reframe turns this from “buy our database” into “here's your existing territory, ranked, including which of your competitor's current customers are displaceable” — a working artifact (the Portland build) exists to demo live, not a slide-deck claim.
