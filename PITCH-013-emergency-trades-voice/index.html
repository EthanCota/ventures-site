24/7 emergency-call capture for trades

The call you missed at 2am was the one worth the most.

IronLine answers every after-hours call for plumbing, electrical, and HVAC shops — triages the urgency, quotes the call-out window, books it into your dispatch software, and texts the on-call tech before the caller hangs up. Built to sit on top of Housecall Pro or Jobber, not replace them.

Caller: "My water heater's leaking all over the garage, it's 1am, I need someone NOW."
IronLine: "I hear you — first, is water near any electrical panel or outlet?"
Caller: "No, it's just pooling on the concrete."
IronLine: "Good. I can get a tech out within the hour. There's a $150 after-hours dispatch fee on top of the repair — want me to book it?"

How it runs the call

Not a general answering service — a voice agent that knows trades triage.

Triage

Urgency triage

Asks the same safety questions a dispatcher would (gas smell, active flooding, sparking) before quoting a window.

Quote

Call-out quoting

States the after-hours premium up front so there's no surprise at the door — matches your posted rates.

Dispatch

Dispatch booking

Books directly into Housecall Pro / Jobber where integration access allows — otherwise hands off a structured summary for manual entry.

On-call tech alert

Texts or calls the tech on rotation the moment a job is booked, with address, callback number, and hazard flags.

Recorded & consented

Opens every call with a recording-consent line — built for California's all-party consent law from day one.

Escalation to a human

Anything outside the script — active gas leak, structure fire risk — escalates straight to a live person, no agent guessing.

Straight answers

The five questions this pitch has to answer honestly.

Why not just use Avoca or Sameday — they already do this?

Avoca is a funded, sales-gated, enterprise-motion competitor with a reported $125M Series B at a $1B valuation and no public pricing — built for buyers much larger than a 1–5-truck shop. Sameday AI is the closer comparable: Launch runs a genuine $449/mo, and Scale visibly starts at $789/mo on Sameday's own page (its schema data separately, inconsistently, still says $449 for Scale) . IronLine's answer is price — Starter undercuts Launch, Growth undercuts Scale's real visible price by more than a flat-$449 comparison would suggest — plus a direct relationship instead of a self-serve signup funnel.

Will this actually pay for itself?

For plumbing and electrical, yes — one captured emergency call's after-hours premium alone covers 29–86% of Starter's monthly cost , before counting the trip fee or labor the shop would've earned anyway. For HVAC, this can't be answered with a number yet — the premium wasn't sourced this pass (unverified). Full math is retained in the provenance record.

Is recording these calls even legal?

Yes, with a consent notice. California Penal Code §632(a) requires all-party consent for confidential communications, fines up to $2,500 per violation — IronLine opens every call with a disclosure line. Clients outside California need their own state check first; the 50-state map isn't resolved yet.

Can this actually talk to my ServiceTitan / Jobber / Housecall Pro?

Pricing and reachability for Jobber and Housecall Pro are confirmed ; actual API-access terms for any of the three are not yet confirmed (unverified). Honest answer today: integration is planned, terms aren't confirmed — not a yes.

What about HVAC specifically — worth it for AC-in-heat calls?

Can't be answered with a number yet. Six sourcing attempts this pass failed to find an HVAC emergency-ticket premium (unverified) — this is the single most important open research item before this goes further.
