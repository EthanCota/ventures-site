Why this matters right now

Fewer buyers are ever reaching a page of blue links to click.

68% zero-click

68.01% of US Google searches in early 2026 ended with no click at all, up from 60.45% in 2024 (SparkToro / Search Engine Land analysis of Similarweb clickstream data, fetched 2026-07-29).

83% on AI answers

Searches that trigger an AI Overview run about 83% zero-click, and only ~1% of AI Overview views produce a click to a cited source (Pew Research Center, "Do people click on links in Google AI summaries?", March 2025 browsing-panel data, published 2025-07-22, fetched 2026-07-29).

A real pricing gap

Local SEO for a true single-location small business runs $100–$800/mo. AI-answer visibility is currently sold either as $1,500+/mo enterprise SaaS or bundled into $750–$3,000/mo retainers. There's no clean, cheap option built for a one-location shop.

Where we draw the line

The compliance surface for this business is narrow, and we'd rather over-explain it than smooth it over.

FTC review rule

Reviews: we don't touch them the risky way. The FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465, finalized August 2024, effective October 21, 2024) carries civil penalties up to $51,744 per violation for fake or incentivized reviews, review-gating, or undisclosed insider reviews. — corroborated via FTC's own press release, the Federal Register listing, and a law-firm summary; the underlying regulatory text itself has not yet been directly quote-matched, so treat the citation as high-confidence secondary, not primary-verified, until that pass is done.

Google GBP policy

Google's own Fake Engagement policy (support.google.com/business/answer/14114287, directly fetched and quoted 2026-07-29) is the day-to-day operating constraint: a profile found in violation can have new reviews blocked, existing reviews unpublished, and a warning label applied — that's the client's listing at risk, not just ours.

Licensing

No professional-licensing regime applies to this business itself — it's a marketing service, not a regulated profession. The compliance surface is entirely the review/advertising side above.

The questions people actually ask

How is this different from the spam "your website needs work" emails I get every week?

Transparency and scope. You get a paid, itemized audit ($250–$400, one-time, no retainer commitment) with the underlying findings shown, not a vague scare pitch. And a public compliance stance — see below — that the mass-email pattern doesn't have.

What is AEO, actually — is this a real thing or a buzzword?

68% of Google searches now end without a click (SparkToro/Search Engine Land), and searches that trigger an AI Overview run around 83% zero-click, with only ~1% of AI Overview views producing a click to a cited source (Pew Research Center, March 2025 panel data, published 2025-07-22). AEO is the response to that shift: getting cited inside an AI-generated answer matters because fewer buyers ever reach a page of results to click a link at all.

How much does this cost and what do I actually get?

Three tiers, shown above, anchored below the small-business-specific market range on purpose. The one-time audit is a low-commitment way to see the findings before you commit to a retainer.

Will you buy or manipulate my Google reviews?

No. We will never solicit, incentivize, gate, or filter your reviews. That's not a disclaimer, it's the point — a vendor that manipulates your GBP risks getting YOUR listing suspended, not just their own reputation.

How do I know this pays for itself?

The breakeven line above: roughly one additional booked job a month at a typical local-service ticket size covers the Foundational tier. Stated plainly as modeled reasoning, not a guarantee.
