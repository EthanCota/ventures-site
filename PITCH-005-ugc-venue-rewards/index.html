Who it's for

Independent San Diego bars & restaurants people already photograph

Built for independently-owned San Diego bars and restaurants (single location, or a small local group of 2-5) that:

  • hold a California on-sale alcohol license,
  • already have a visually distinctive space or signature item patrons photograph unprompted — a rooftop view, a neon sign, a dish or drink with a name,
  • spend little or nothing today on paid loyalty/UGC tooling — running Instagram and word-of-mouth manually rather than a subscription platform.

If that's not you yet — if nobody's tagging you unprompted — Fareloop isn't the right first move; fix the photogenic hook first.

The operator panel — straight talk

What venues actually get, and what we didn't build yet

Verification queue

Every submitted post runs through geofenced location checks plus a manual review pass in the early cohort — fraud is Fareloop's hardest problem, and MVP handles it by hand at small volume, not by pretending it's solved with a model.

Built-in FTC disclosure

Every rewarded post gets an auto-appended disclosure line in the caption suggestion — required by FTC endorsement guidance, never left to the patron to remember.

Venue dashboard

See redemptions, top content, and cross-venue traffic your network partners are sending you (once networked).

Cross-venue token network

Food/merch tokens redeemable at any participating partner venue — the differentiator vs. a single-venue giveaway.

Redemption caps

Per-patron weekly caps blunt reward-farming without needing a fraud model on day one.

Referral layer

Hospitality groups that bring in another venue group get a 10% recurring discount — the network grows through people who already trust each other.

What we deliberately did NOT build into the MVP

  • No cross-venue alcohol comps. The idea's original pitch — content at Venue A redeems for a drink at Venue B — sits in an unresolved zone of CA alcohol law. We scoped it out rather than sell you something we can't stand behind legally.
  • No automated fraud detection, yet. Manual review at small scale, honestly, until volume justifies real tooling.
  • No claim of platform-policy clearance (Yelp, Instagram/Meta branded-content rules) — that layer needs its own confirmation before scale.

Objections

The 5 questions every venue owner asks first

Isn't giving away free drinks for social posts illegal in California?
For alcohol specifically — partially unresolved, and we're not hiding that. CA law (B&P Code §25600.1(a)(5)) bars alcohol as a contest prize outright. A single venue comping its own drinks to its own patrons has real cover under §25600.3(d). That's why Fareloop's MVP rewards food and merchandise across venues, and only ever offers alcohol comps — if at all — within one venue's own funded program. We are not selling you the riskiest version of this idea.
Do I have to disclose that patrons got something free for posting?
Yes, per FTC guidance directly on point for restaurants: free meals in exchange for social content require disclosure. Fareloop auto-appends a disclosure line to every rewarded post's caption suggestion — built in, not left to the patron to remember.
How is this different from just running my own Instagram giveaway?
A giveaway is one-shot and stays inside your own audience. Fareloop's cross-venue token model lets patrons discover partner venues through your content and vice versa — though honestly, that networking value only shows up once multiple venues are live. One venue alone is closer to a comped-content tool than a network effect.
What stops people from posting fake or low-quality content just to farm free stuff?
This is the hardest problem in the product, and we're not claiming it's solved. MVP mitigation is manual review + geofencing + per-patron weekly redemption caps — small enough cohorts to review by hand while real fraud tooling gets built.
Why would I pay $79-199/mo when I could just ask happy customers to tag us for free?
You can, and plenty of venues already do. Fareloop's pitch is structure and reach: verified attribution, cross-venue discovery, and a lower guaranteed content cost than the $25-110/video UGC-marketplace floor — funded in comped product at marginal cost, not cash. If your organic posting culture is already strong and you don't want cross-venue exposure, you're a weak fit — we'd rather tell you that than oversell it.
